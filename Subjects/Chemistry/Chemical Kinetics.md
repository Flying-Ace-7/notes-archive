For any reaction, with product(s), $A$ and reactant(s), $B$
$$
A \rightarrow B
$$
$$
\text{rate} = \frac{\Delta \text{products}}{\Delta t} = \frac{d[B]}{dt}

$$
or $$
\text{rate}= - \frac{\Delta \mathrm{reactants}}{\Delta t}=-\frac{d[A]}{dt}
$$

For a reaction equation with stoichiometric coefficients:
> such as $$aA\to bB$$

$$
\text{rate} = -\frac{1}{a} \frac{\Delta A}{\Delta t}= \frac{1}{b} \frac{\Delta B}{\Delta T}
$$
## Rate laws
Reaction order is always determined experimentally.

A first order reaction w.r.t to A has the following:
$$
\text{rate}= k[A]
$$
second order
$$
\text{rate} = k [A]^2
$$
Zero order
$$
\text{rate} = k
$$
(rate is independant of concentration of A)

## Integrated rate laws:

First order reaction:
$$
\ln [A]_{t}=\ln[A]_{0}-kt
$$
Second order reaction:
$$
\frac{1}{[A]_{t}}= \frac{1}{[A]_{0}}+kt
$$
Zero-order reaction:
$$
[A]_{t}=[A]_{0} - kt
$$
Where:
$[A]_t$ is the concentration of a reactant at a certain time, $t$ 
$[A]_0$ is the initiall concentration of a reactant
k is the reaction constant

## Arrehenius equation
$$
\ln\left( \frac{k_{1}}{k_{2}} \right)=-\frac{E_{a}}{R}\left( \frac{1}{T_{1}}-\frac{1}{T_{2}} \right)
$$
$E_a$ is the activation energy. This equation is used ot compare two reaction constants at different temperatures at which reactions take place.

### Units of k
IN first order: $s^{-1}$
second order: $M^{-1} s^{-1}$
Zero order: $M s^{-1}$
