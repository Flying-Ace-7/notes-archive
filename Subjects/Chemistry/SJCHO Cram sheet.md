## Thermochemistry

we look at the laws of thermodynamics as follows:
[[Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics]]
### First law
[[First Law of Thermodynamics]]:
$$
\Delta E = q + W
$$
where, $\Delta E$ represents the change in the internal energy of the gas. q, heat gained, W, work done on the gas.
This is seen as kinda trivial.

Work can be done by the gas on the environment when it expands . it results in the following equation:
$$
W = -\rho\Delta V
$$
If the gas expands, work is _done on the environment_. If it contracts, work _is done on the gas_.

(Bonus) If pressure not constant:
$$
W = \int ^{v_{2}}_{v_{1}}\rho  dV 
$$

## Enthalpy and Entropy
### Enthalpy
It is defined as the internal energy + product of pressure and volume:
$$
H = E + \rho V

$$

Since,:
$$
\Delta E = q - \rho\Delta V
$$
where q is the heat energy supplied to te system at **constant pressure.**

Hence, : $$
H = q
$$
change in enthalpy is the same as the amount of heat supplied to the gas.
Heat change in a substance can be defined as:
$$
q=mc\Delta T
$$
where m is the mass of the substance, c is the specific heat capacity of the substance($Jg^{-1 }K^{-1}$) , and T is the change in temp

If $\Delta H>0$, it is and <mark class="hltr-red">endothermic</mark> reaction, heat is absorbed from surroundings. If $\Delta H<0$, the reaction is <mark class="hltr-blue">exothermic</mark>, and heat is released into the surroundings

$$
\Delta H^\circ_{reaction} = \sum \Delta H_{bonds\ broken\ (reactants)} - \sum \Delta H_{bonds\ formed\ (products)}

$$
