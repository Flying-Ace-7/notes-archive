 COMMENTS:

The stand needs to be much clearer. For a question such as “Is this true of your society?”, the reader should know very early whether the writer believes that young people do, do not, or partially have it better. EG,

Overall, I believe that young people in Singapore do have better lives than previous generations because they enjoy greater access to technology, education and opportunities. However, this does not mean that their lives are necessarily easier, as they also face intense academic and social pressures.

The interpretation of “have it so good” needs to be broader. This is the most important area for improvement. The essay focuses heavily on:

- AI making homework easier
- online gaming
- Snapchat streaks
- Instagram/TikTok
- communicating with friends

These are examples of convenience and leisure, but they are relatively narrow interpretations of quality of life. A stronger response should consider broader areas such as:

- Living standards: better housing, healthcare, nutrition and access to services
- Education: greater access to education and learning resources
- Technology: convenience, information and communication
- Safety and security: Singapore's political and physical stability
- Opportunities: greater opportunities for women, minorities and people with different talents
- Healthcare and life expectancy: improvements compared with previous generations
- Work and future prospects: whether young people actually have better economic opportunities
- Mental health and competition: whether modern pressures offset these advantages

Be careful with examples that contradict your argument. The Muhammad Darzley Abil example is used to demonstrate the benefits of technology, but the details presented show addiction, isolation, excessive screen use etc. That makes it a poor example for proving that young people “have it so good”.

ALSO: The essay needs more direct comparison with previous generations. This is the biggest missing feature. The question isn't simply:

Do young people have good lives?

It is: ‘Do young people today have better lives than young people in the past?’

Therefore, comparisons should appear throughout the essay:

Unlike previous generations...  
 In the past, young people had fewer opportunities to...  
 Although earlier generations faced fewer pressures in this area...  
 Compared with their parents' generation...
