## THEME 1: DIGITAL COMMUNICATION AND YOUTH

---

### SECTION A: Possible Essay Questions

#### Discursive Essay Questions

| # | Question |
|---|---|
| 1 | "Digital communication has enriched the lives of young people more than it has harmed them." Discuss. |
| 2 | To what extent has digital communication redefined the nature of friendship and community for today's youth? |
| 3 | Evaluate the impact of social media on the formation of identity in young people. |
| 4 | "The rise of digital communication has created a generation that is more connected, yet more isolated than ever before." Discuss this paradox. |

#### Argumentative Essay Questions

| #   | Question                                                                                                                                          |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | "Governments and tech companies must do more to regulate social media to protect the mental health of young people." To what extent do you agree? |
| 2   | "Parents should have the right to monitor all of their children's digital communications." Argue for or against this statement.                   |
| 3   | "The negative impact of digital communication on youth greatly outweighs its benefits." Write an argument in support of this view.                |
| 4   | "Schools should ban smartphones entirely." Argue your position on this topic.                                                                     |

---

### SECTION B: Key Arguments and Evidence

**Overall Stance:** The negative impacts on youth currently outweigh the positives.

---

#### POINT 1: Supporting Argument

| Component      | Details                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Argument**   | The unfettered use of digital communication, particularly social media, is a primary driver of the current adolescent mental health crisis, fueling anxiety, depression, and poor body image.                                                                         |
| **Evidence 1** | A 2022 study by **Cambridge University**, analyzing over 84,000 people, found a direct link between social media use and a decline in life satisfaction among certain age groups, with the effect being most pronounced during adolescence.                           |
| **Evidence 2** | A 2021 study published in ***The Lancet Child & Adolescent Health*** found a strong correlation between increased social media use and higher rates of body image dissatisfaction and eating disorders among teenage girls.                                           |
| **Evidence 3** | Data from the **American College Health Association (ACHA)** has shown a significant surge in students seeking mental health services for anxiety and depression over the last decade, a trend that closely mirrors the rise in smartphone and social media adoption. |

---

#### POINT 2: Supporting Argument

| Component | Details |
|---|---|
| **Argument** | Over-reliance on text-based and asynchronous communication is atrophying crucial social skills (reading non-verbal cues, conflict resolution) and cognitive abilities (deep focus, critical thinking) in young people. |
| **Evidence 1** | A 2017 study in the journal ***Educational Psychology*** found that merely having a smartphone visible on a desk reduced a student's cognitive capacity, even when it was turned off—a phenomenon known as the "brain drain" effect. |
| **Evidence 2** | Employers and university professors consistently report that new cohorts of young adults, while technologically savvy, often struggle with face-to-face negotiation, handling spontaneous conflict, and engaging in sustained deep work. |
| **Evidence 3** | Research from the **Cyberbullying Research Center** indicates that approximately 37% of young people between the ages of 12 and 17 have been bullied online, a form of conflict that often lacks the social cues and immediate consequences that would help teach appropriate behavior and resolution. |

---

#### POINT 3: Counter-Argument

| Component | Details |
|---|---|
| **Argument** | Digital communication provides a vital and often life-saving lifeline for marginalized youth, offering community, support, and a sense of belonging that is unavailable in their physical environment. |
| **Evidence 1** | A 2019 study published in the ***Journal of Medical Internet Research*** found that LGBTQ+ youth who used social media to connect with peers reported lower levels of depression and anxiety. |
| **Evidence 2** | Online communities allow youth with niche interests or those living in isolated rural areas to find like-minded peers, fostering social validation and reducing feelings of profound loneliness. |
| **Evidence 3** | Digital platforms have been instrumental in organizing youth-led social movements (e.g., climate strikes, social justice campaigns), demonstrating their power as a tool for positive civic engagement and empowerment. |

## THEME 2: OVERPROTECTION OF YOUTH

---

### SECTION A: Possible Essay Questions

#### Discursive Essay Questions

| # | Question |
|---|---|
| 1 | To what extent is the modern culture of "safetyism" harmful to the development of young people? |
| 2 | "Overprotection is a form of neglect." Discuss this statement in the context of raising children. |
| 3 | Evaluate the social and economic factors that have contributed to the rise of "helicopter parenting." |
| 4 | What is the ideal balance between protecting children from harm and allowing them the freedom to fail? Discuss. |

#### Argumentative Essay Questions

| # | Question |
|---|---|
| 1 | "The overprotection of youth by parents and institutions is creating a crisis of resilience." Argue your position. |
| 2 | "It is better to prepare the child for the road, not the road for the child." Write an argument in favor of this statement. |
| 3 | "Modern parenting's focus on safety and success is doing more harm than good." To what extent do you agree? |
| 4 | "Schools and governments should implement policies that actively encourage 'risky play' for children." Argue for or against this proposal. |

---

### SECTION B: Key Arguments and Evidence

**Overall Stance:** Overprotection is significantly harming youth.

---

#### POINT 1: Supporting Argument

| Component | Details |
|---|---|
| **Argument** | Overprotection prevents young people from experiencing failure, frustration, and discomfort, which are essential building blocks for developing psychological resilience and emotional regulation. |
| **Evidence 1** | A 2018 study published in the journal ***Development and Psychopathology*** found that children with over-controlling parents were significantly less able to manage their emotions and behavior by age five, with this deficit widening into adolescence. |
| **Evidence 2** | Psychologists have identified a phenomenon called **"learned helplessness,"** where individuals who are repeatedly spared from solving their own problems lose the belief in their own ability to succeed, a direct outcome of overprotective parenting. |
| **Evidence 3** | The work of **Professor Ellen Sandseter** at Queen Maud University in Norway shows that children deprived of thrilling and risky play are more likely to develop anxiety disorders later in life, as they lack the experience of successfully navigating fear. |

---

#### POINT 2: Supporting Argument

| Component | Details |
|---|---|
| **Argument** | By acting as "snowplows" and clearing all obstacles, overprotective parents and institutions foster a dependency mindset, leaving young adults unable to function independently or solve problems on their own. |
| **Evidence 1** | A study by the **University of British Columbia** found that "helicopter parenting" was directly linked to lower levels of autonomy, competence, and relatedness in young adults—the three basic psychological needs identified in Self-Determination Theory. |
| **Evidence 2** | A 2016 report from the **American College Health Association** highlighted a surge in students seeking help for anxiety, with many citing an inability to cope with the pressures of independent living, a direct consequence of a highly controlled upbringing. |
| **Evidence 3** | University professors and HR managers frequently report that young adults now struggle with basic tasks like making appointments, managing conflicts with a peer, or completing projects without explicit, step-by-step instructions, indicating a deficit in self-efficacy. |

---

#### POINT 3: Counter-Argument

| Component | Details |
|---|---|
| **Argument** | Increased protection is a rational and logical response to the unique and complex dangers of the modern world, such as cyberbullying, online predators, and an increasingly competitive global economy. |
| **Evidence 1** | The nature of bullying has changed, with cyberbullying being relentless and invasive. The statistic of 37% of youth experiencing cyberbullying (from the Cyberbullying Research Center) justifies why parents feel the need to monitor their children's online lives. |
| **Evidence 2** | The cost of failure has increased. A parent who intervenes to help their child with a project or a university application is responding to the real, high-stakes competition for future educational and economic opportunities. |
| **Evidence 3** | The pervasive and easily accessible nature of harmful content (violence, pornography, misinformation) online means that parents are facing threats to their children's development that previous generations could not have imagined, making a "protective" stance seem not only reasonable but necessary. |

---

### SECTION D: How to Use the Counter-Argument

The counter-argument is a powerful rhetorical tool. You should not just state it; you must **refute** or **acknowledge and minimize** it to strengthen your own position.

| Theme | How to Refute the Counter-Argument |
|---|---|
| **Digital Communication** | "While this is a profoundly important benefit, it does not require an unfettered digital world. The challenge is to create spaces for these vital communities to thrive while mitigating the harms to the general population. The solution is not abolition, but thoughtful regulation and education, which is precisely the argument being made." |
| **Overprotection** | "These fears are valid, but the solution is not to eliminate all risk, which is impossible and counterproductive. The more effective approach is to equip young people with the skills to navigate a dangerous world—digital literacy, critical thinking, and resilience—which is the very thing overprotection prevents." |
