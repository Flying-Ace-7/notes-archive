### Structure of Spinal Cord
![[Cross-Section of Spinal cord.png]]


The bony vertebrate column protects the spinal cord, which is a bundle or cords that conveys information to and from the brain. Actd independantly from the brain to integrate sensory signals and generate motor signals to produce reflexes.

- Grey matter
	- Consists of the cell bodies of motor neurons and the interneurons
- White matter
	- constist of axons of neurons

In the brain, the white matter is the inner layer, while the grey matter is the outer layer.
In the spinal cord, the grey mayyer is the inner layer, while the white matter is the outer layer.

The central canal runs along the length of the spinal cord, and contains cerebrospinalfluid (CSF) formed in the brain through filtration of arterial blood. It supplies nutrients and oxygen to CNS cells and transport away the waste materials.

Dorsal root, contains the axons of sensory neurons, and the dorsal root ganglion contains the cell bodies of sensory neurons.

The ventral root contains the axon of motor neurons, and the cell bodies of motor neurons are located within the grey matter


### 2 types of Spinal reflexes

- Monosynaptic reflexes
	- Simple relfex
	- Only one synapse
	- No internuerons
- Polysnaptic reflexes
	- Complex reflex
	- Multiple synapse
	- Interneurons involved
	- Passes info to the brain for pain perception and regsitering as a memory

> [!warning] Note that not all reflexes have the spinal cord as integration centre, some reflexes such as the pupil reflex have their integreation centre at the brain
