First, we shall understand how trichromatic and monchromatic visions work

### Trichromatic vision
Cones produce trichromatic vision, and there are 3 types of the cones. (RGB)
Each cone has 1 different type of pigment.
They absorb overlapping ranges of wavelengths, but each of them peak at diferent values
The brain integrates the signals form the three types of cones, and compares the ratio of of the simulation, to percieve differenr colours

### Monochromatic vision
Only one type of rod and pigment,absorbing a wide range of wavelgths.
Only one peak sensitivity.
No ratio comparison -> greyscale vision
<mark class="hltr-blue">Perception based on difference of light intensity</mark>

> [!question] Do blue and green appear equally bright at night
> No.
> The peak sensitivity of rods is closer to green than blue, hence the rods favour green. More green light is absorbed by the rod than the blue light, **green looks brighter**


## Colour blindness (finally)

It is caused by a deficiency in one or more cone types.

Red-Green colour blindness from anamolies in the red or green cones
