Protists is the informal name of the kingdom that are **mostly** unicellular eukaryotes that are neither:
- Plants
- Animals, nor
- Fungi

Protists are hard to classify.
_Domain Protista_ is polyphyletic, where members are descended from different ancestors.
They can either be autotrophic, Heterotrophic, or mixotrophic, plant-like, animal-like or fungus like respectively.
They can reproduce asexually or sexually.
They are a wide range of organisms.

Some protists reproduce sexually, through [Meiosis]([[Heredity I#Meiosis]]) or fertilisation. Some examples include the conjugation of paramecium, which is a sexual process.

## Protist evolution
### Endosymbiosis
Much of protist diversity has its origins in endosymbiosis.
Endosymbiosis -> process in which certain unicellular organisms engulf other cells, which become endosymbionts and ultimately organelles in host cells. Some examples inculde chloroplasts and mitochondrion in plant and animal cells respectively.

Mitochondira orginated from an aerobic prokaryote becoming an endosymbiont in a larger prokaryote.
Chloroplats evolved from cyanobacterium, becoming and endosymbiont in a larger prokrayote

#### Example: Hatena artenicola
It is a micro-organism that has a complex feeding apparatus to obtain food. Through endosymbiosis, it engulfs algae, which photosynthesises and provides food for itself and the hatena arenciola. _Hatnea_ stops feeding, and the feeding apparatus changes into a light-sensing organelle, guiding them to light for faster rate of photosynthesis. 
During reproduction through binary fission, the _Hatena_ splits, and the algae remains in only one of the daughter cells. The other cells remains as a predator, until it engulfs another algae.


### Secondary endosymbiosis

A cynobacterium was engulfed by a heterotrophic eukaryote (primary endosymbiosis) to produce and ancestor with chloroplasts which diversified into:
- red algae
- green algae
Red and green algae were subsequently engulfed by other eukaryotes (Secondary endosymbiosis), which resulted in other types of protists, who where Mixotrophs.



### Four super gropus of of all eukaryotes
Our current hypothesis on the evolution of protists groups changes rapidly. Our current hypothesis divides all eukaryotes (including protists) into four supergroups:
- **Excavata**
- **SAR clade**
- **Archaeplastida**
- **Unikonta**

## Plant-like protists


- Autothrophic.
- Can be single-celled or multi-cellular
- Single-Celled: phytoplankton
- Multicellular: algae

6 major phyla:
1. Pyrrophyta (Dinoflagellates)
	1. dinoflagellate blooms make coastal water appear pink or brownish red
	2. Presence of Carotenoids in dinofllagelate plastids
	3. Some dinoflagellates produce toxins
	4. These blooms are called "harmful algal blooms"
	5. They are ingested by herbivorous fish, and get concentrated un large carnivorous fish in liver, intestines, roe and head.
	
2. Chrysophyta (Diatoms)
	1. Chryso: like crystal
	2. Known as pastures of the sea
	3. Diatoms consist of around 11 000 species.
		1. Have protective shells made of silica
	4. Unicellular or form colonies
		1. Colonies often look like stars
		2. Average cell size: 60-80 micrometers long, 2-4 micrometeres wide.
	5. <mark class="hltr-green">All are autotrophic </mark>and photsynthesize, from which they get food and water
	6. Found in both freshwater and marine environments
	7. Abundance in the past is evident in the fossil record
	8. _Diatoms are enclosed by cell walls, spines made of silica, to protect itself from crushing jaws of predators, withstand high pressure_
	9. Responsibel for 20-25 % of carbon fixation, 40% of marine primary productivity.
	10. Eaten by protists and invertebrates
3. Euglenophyta (Euglena)
	1. Complex unicellular organism
	2. Has a single visible flagellum for locomotion
	3. Flexible outer covering called a pellicle (allows itself to wriggle unlike dinoflagellets and diatoms)
	4. Has an eyespot, allowing them to sense and move towards light.
	5. <mark class="hltr-green">Autotrophs</mark>
	6. Accumulates water and expels it through cell membrane
4. RhodoPhyta (red algae)
5. Phaephyta (Brown algae)
	1. Phaeo means grey or dusky
	2. Inhabit only marine environmenrs, where the majority live in cold ocean water
	3. Strictly **Multicellular**
	4. Large protists can form dense kelp forests
	5. The lifecycle of the brown alga
		1. The diploid generation is called a sporophyte, the haploid generation is called a gametophyte
		2. The sporophyte produces haploid spored, called zoospores in the brown algae _Laminaria_.
		3. Spores develop into multicellular haploid male and femle gametophytes, which produce gametes.
		4. Fertilisation of gametes results in a diploid zygote, which grows into a new sporophyte.
